About the atlas

A quiet archive for loud histories.

Fossil Atlas is designed as an educational starting point. Each profile combines a commonly cited extinction date with habitat, range, diet, size, causes of decline, and a short historical note.

Important: extinction dates can be debated, especially for prehistoric species and animals whose last individuals may have survived unnoticed. “Extinct” here means no living population is known to remain.

Read the dates carefully

Some species have a precise final-record date, such as the last known passenger pigeon in 1914. Others are necessarily approximate—for example, many Pleistocene megafauna disappeared thousands of years ago and cannot be assigned a single calendar year.

For a production research site, pair each entry with primary literature, IUCN assessments, museum collections, and fossil records.
